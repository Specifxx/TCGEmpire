# Outreach kit — the partnership channel

Written 2026-09-21, as the working half of `docs/MARKETING-PLAN.md`.

`docs/PROMO-KIT.md` is for **broadcasting** — posts you publish yourself. This is
for **asking**: three email templates, who to send them to, and what to do when
somebody replies. It exists because the promo kit's own closing note has been
saying the quiet part for months:

> real backlinks from those Riftbound sites do more for traffic + Google ranking
> than any single post. Prioritise the partnership emails.

Nobody has sent one. This is the channel the site has never used, and the one
both that note and the outside SEO review (2026-09-21) rank first.

---

## Why this works when creator outreach didn't

Creator outreach was tried and produced replies that went nowhere. The diagnosis
is not that the people were wrong — it's that the email asked for a favour and
offered nothing. Every template below leads with something the recipient gets to
keep, and none of them costs anything to give:

| Who | What they get | What we ask for |
|---|---|---|
| A tracked store | Their private repricing report — every listing of theirs that another shop beats | That when they say "we price match", the words link here |
| A community site | A live widget (price badge, market index, release countdown) their readers want | Nothing beyond using it |
| A creator | Free price research for whatever they're making | Nothing at all |

None of these asks a creator to weigh anything up, which is the part that made
the earlier round go nowhere.

**Anchor text is always the brand or a plain phrase** — "RiftCompare", "compare
prices", "price check". Never an exact-match keyword string, never a paid link,
never a guest-post farm. The consultant's advice was three or four brand-only
links a month and patience, and patience is the part that is actually hard:
links earned in October show up in rankings in November and December.

## The cadence

**One email, Tuesday, 25 minutes.** That is the entire commitment. Twelve weeks,
twelve emails, and the target is six to eight new referring domains — at least
three of them Riftbound-specific.

Rotate in this order, because it runs warmest-first:

1. **Weeks 1–6: stores.** They already benefit from us (we send them customers),
   the free report is concrete, and the same email is the one that surfaces the
   $250 consulting session. Work the list at `/admin/store-partners`, which shows
   the biggest tracked stores that have never been contacted.
2. **Weeks 7–9: community sites.** RiftDecks, Piltover Archive, RiftMana. Lead
   with the widget, not with us.
3. **Weeks 10–12: creators.** Offer the research, not a partnership.

Track it in one spreadsheet with five columns: **Who · Email · Sent · Replied ·
Outcome**. That is the whole CRM and it does not need to be anything more.

**One follow-up, ever, seven days later, two sentences.** Then stop. A second
follow-up to a small shop is how you become the person they don't open.

## Template 1 — a tracked store

**Send this after** creating their report link at `/admin/store-partners`
(pick the store, click *Create report link*, copy it). Check the listing count is
not zero first; the page shows it, and an empty report is worse than no report.

**Subject:** `Where {Store} is being undercut on Riftbound — free report`

> Hi {Name},
>
> I run RiftCompare, a free Riftbound price-comparison site. {Store} is one of
> the {N} stores we compare, so your listings are already in it and customers
> click through to you from us.
>
> I've put together a private report showing every Riftbound card where another
> shop currently beats you on price, and by how much. It's yours, free, no strings
> — I built it because it's genuinely useful to the stores I track:
>
> {report link}
>
> One small thing, if you find it useful: a lot of shops tell customers they price
> match or are competitive. If you say that anywhere on your site, linking the
> words "compare prices" to riftcompare.com makes the claim checkable, and sends
> your customers to a page where you're one of the shops being compared.
>
> Either way the report is yours to keep. Happy to answer anything.
>
> {Your name}
> riftcompare.com

**Why it's shaped this way.** The value arrives before the ask. The ask is one
sentence, buried, and framed as something that helps *them* substantiate a claim
they already make. There is no deadline, no "let me know either way", and no
mention of consulting — that comes up only if they reply.

## Template 2 — a community site

**Targets:** RiftDecks, Piltover Archive, RiftMana, plus any Riftbound wiki, deck
database or news site you come across. Look for a contact page, a Discord with a
staff channel, or the site owner's X account.

**Subject:** `A free live-price widget for {Site}, if it's useful`

> Hi,
>
> {Site} is one of the few places doing Riftbound properly, so I'll keep this
> short.
>
> I run RiftCompare — we compare live Riftbound prices across {N} stores in six
> markets. We've built three widgets that any site can embed with one line of
> HTML, free, no signup, no tracking, and they're all marked noindex so they can't
> compete with your own pages in search:
>
> - a live price badge for any single card ("from $X across N stores")
> - the Riftbound market index, with a sparkline
> - a countdown to the next set release
>
> They're all here with copy-paste snippets: riftcompare.com/embed
>
> The countdown one might be the most useful to you right now, with Radiance
> landing on 23 October. No obligation and no catch — if a widget would be more
> useful in a shape we haven't built, tell me and I'll look at building it.
>
> {Your name}
> riftcompare.com

**During preview season, add this line** before the sign-off:

> We're also logging every official Radiance reveal as it lands, with prices
> appearing on each card as shops list it — riftcompare.com/blog/riftbound-radiance-spoilers
> — if that's useful to link or to check against.

## Template 3 — a creator

**Read this first.** `/creators` is a list of *confirmed partnerships* — the page
is headed "Socials & Creator Partners" and the data behind it is typed as such.
Adding someone unasked so you can tell them they're listed would be claiming a
relationship that does not exist, on a public page. Don't. It is also the one
version of this email that could go badly in a community this small.

So the creator email gives something real instead: **free research**. A creator
making a "most expensive Radiance cards" video, a budget-deck video or a set
review has a data problem, and that is the thing worth solving for nothing.

**Subject:** `Free Riftbound price data, if it's ever useful to you`

> Hi {Name},
>
> I run RiftCompare — a free Riftbound price-comparison site that tracks live
> prices across {N} stores in six markets.
>
> No pitch, and nothing I want from you. I just wanted you to know that if you're
> ever making something where the numbers matter — most expensive cards in a set,
> what a deck actually costs to build, how a card's price moved after an event —
> I'm happy to pull that data for you and send it over. It takes me minutes and
> the site is the only place that has some of it.
>
> There are also embeddable widgets if they're ever useful for a blog post or a
> description: riftcompare.com/embed
>
> That's it. Good luck with {the thing they're working on}.
>
> {Your name}
> riftcompare.com

**Fill in that last blank properly.** A specific reference to something they
actually made is the difference between this and a mail merge, and it is the only
part of the email that takes real time.

**Do not** ask for a shout-out, a video, or a link. If a creator does want an
ongoing partnership, `/creators` is where a confirmed one gets listed — and by
then they will have said yes, which is the whole difference.

## When somebody replies

**A store says thanks and nothing else.** Reply once, briefly, and ask a real
question: *"Out of interest — how do you set your Riftbound prices at the moment?"*
That answer is worth more than the link. It is also exactly the conversation the
$250 session exists to have, so if they describe a problem you can solve in an
hour, say so plainly and send `/stores/consulting`. Do not lead with it.

**A store asks to be removed from the comparison.** Remove them, immediately and
without arguing. Say it's done and that they're welcome back any time.

**A store asks for better placement, or offers to pay for it.** No — and say why
in one line: the ranking is by price, that is the only reason anyone trusts it,
and a store that could buy its way up would make the site worthless to the buyers
we'd be sending them. This is not negotiable and it is the same answer the
consulting page already gives. A paid session never affects ranking.

**A community site says yes.** Send the snippet, offer to check it renders, and
then leave them alone.

**Nobody replies.** Normal. A 10–20% reply rate on cold email to small businesses
is a good outcome, so ten stores is one or two conversations. The twelve-week
target assumes most of these go nowhere.

## What not to do

- **Don't buy links, swap links, or write guest posts for the link.** Everything
  above is editorial and would survive being looked at by a human at Google.
- **Don't send the same email twice** with the store name changed and a mistake in
  it. Read it once before sending; a shop owner can tell.
- **Don't contact Riot.** Nothing here is official and nothing should imply it is.
- **Don't mass-mail this.** These are individual emails from a person, sent one a
  week. The bulk email system is for subscribers who asked to hear from us.
- **Don't promise a store more sales.** We don't know that. The report is a fact;
  the outcome isn't. The consulting page's own copy makes the same distinction on
  purpose, and a test enforces it.
